## Design Thinking for Innovation SA 

#### SafePath: Smart System for Lost Individuals Support & Resource Mapping

### Overview 

*The **SafePath** project is designed using the **Design Thinking approach**, which focuses on understanding user challenges and creating practical solutions. By prioritizing a **user-centered approach**, we gathered insights from **elderly individuals, caregivers, and emergency responders** to identify difficulties in navigation and safety concerns.*

*Based on this research, we developed key features such as **voice-guided navigation, an SOS alert system, emergency resource mapping, and caregiver tracking**. The app’s interface was designed to be **simple, accessible, and easy to use**, ensuring that even individuals with limited technical skills can navigate it effortlessly.*  

*Through user testing and feedback, we refined the design to enhance usability and response efficiency. SafePath is now a **reliable navigation and emergency support app**, helping users stay **safe, connected, and in control of their surroundings**.*

### Objective:
*- SafePath is like a helpful friend in your pocket that makes getting around easier and safer, especially for people who might need a little extra help. Think of it as a special phone app that uses maps and your phone's location to guide you.*

*- It's great for older adults, kids, or anyone who sometimes gets confused or lost. SafePath uses GPS, just like the maps app on your phone, to show you where you are and tell you which way to go with easy-to-understand voice directions.*

*- If something goes wrong, like you get lost or need help, there's a special button you can press called SOS. This sends an alert to people you trust, like family or caregivers, so they know you need them.*

*- SafePath can also let your caregivers see where you are, which can be really reassuring for them. It also shows you a map of important places nearby, like hospitals or police stations, in case of an emergency. So, SafePath is all about helping people feel safer and more connected while they're out and about.*


### Target Audience:
*Here's a breakdown of who this product is for:*

*- Older Adults: Especially helpful for seniors who might get confused, wander, or have trouble remembering where they are.*

*- Kids: Good for children who are still learning how to find their way around, especially in new places.*

*- People with Memory Problems: Specifically designed for individuals with conditions like dementia or Alzheimer's who can easily become disoriented or separated from their families.*

*- Caregivers: This product gives peace of mind to people who look after vulnerable individuals, allowing them to keep track of their location and ensure their safety.*

*- Emergency Responders: Useful for police, search and rescue teams, and other first responders when they are looking for missing persons. It helps them find people faster and reunite them with their loved ones.*

### User Persona

#### *User Persona 1: Sarah (Elderly User)*

*- Name: Sarah*

*- Age: 62*

*- Background: Retired teacher, lives alone but has family nearby*

*- Tech Skills: Basic (Uses phone mainly for calls and messaging)*

#### *Behavior & Challenges*

*- Struggles with complex navigation apps and small text.*

*- Feels anxious when traveling alone and avoids unfamiliar places.*

*- Calls her family for help if she gets lost.*

*- Has difficulty reading maps and prefers simple voice guidance.*

#### *Pain Points:*

*- Cannot easily use Google Maps due to small buttons and cluttered UI.*

*- Fear of getting lost and not being able to contact family.*

*- Finds emergency call options confusing in current apps.*

#### *Needs & Goals:*
*- A large-button SOS feature that instantly alerts family members.*

*- Voice-guided step-by-step navigation instead of a complex map.*

*- A simple and easy-to-use interface with big text and high contrast.*

*- An app that automatically shares location with her caregiver when she’s in distress.*

*Key Insight: Sarah needs a user-friendly, voice-guided navigation app with an SOS button and real-time caregiver tracking to feel safe while traveling alone./

### User Research Insights & Survey Summary

#### Overview

*To gain a thorough understanding of the difficulties encountered by individuals who become lost and require emergency navigation assistance, a comprehensive user survey was undertaken. This survey specifically targeted several key stakeholder groups: elderly individuals, who are often more vulnerable in lost situations; caregivers, who provide essential support and possess valuable insights into the needs of those they care for; and rescue volunteers, who are directly involved in search and rescue operations and have firsthand experience with the challenges involved. The primary goal of this research effort was to meticulously identify the specific pain points experienced by these user groups when dealing with lost individuals needing help. This in-depth understanding of the challenges and frustrations experienced was intended to directly inform the design and development of the SafePath app, a smart and innovative system designed to provide comprehensive support for lost individuals. In addition, the app would incorporate resource mapping functionalities, allowing for more efficient and effective allocation of assistance during emergency situations. The ultimate objective was to create a user-centered application that effectively addresses the identified needs and improves the safety and well-being of those who find themselves lost and in need of emergency navigational support.*

#### Key Findings from the Survey

##### *Age Groups of Participants:*

*- Under 18: 2 respondents*

*- 18 - 30: 3 respondents*

*- 31 - 50: 2 respondents*

*- 51 - 70: 3 respondents*

##### *Physical or Cognitive Impairments:*

*- 25% of users reported cognitive or physical impairments affecting navigation.*

##### *User Roles:*

*- 40% were caregivers (taking care of elderly individuals).*

*- 30% were emergency responders/rescuers.*

*- 30% were individuals who had personally experienced getting lost.*

#### Navigation & Safety Challenges

*- 67% of users have felt lost or disoriented in an unfamiliar environment.*

##### *Primary reasons for getting lost:*

*- Poor GPS signal (50%)*

*- Confusing maps or unclear directions (75%)*

*- Cognitive/memory difficulties (40%)*

##### *How users currently find help:*

*- 65% use Google Maps or another navigation app*

*- 45% call family or emergency services*

*- 20% ask people nearby*

##### *Challenges users face when using navigation apps:*

*- Complicated interface with too many buttons (55%)*

*- No emergency SOS feature (40%)*

*- No real-time location updates for caregivers (35%)*

*- Poor accessibility options (text size, voice guidance) (30%)*

#### Most Requested Features

##### *Users ranked these features as most useful in the SafePath app:*

*- SOS Button for Emergency Alerts (85%)*

*- Emergency Resource Mapping (Hospitals, Police, Shelters) (70%)*

*- Caregiver Tracking & Periodic Location Updates (65%)*

*- Step-by-Step Voice Navigation (60%)*

*- Multilingual Support & Accessibility Options (45%)*

#### User Preferences & Accessibility Needs

*- 60% prefer step-by-step voice instructions over complex maps.*

*- 70% want an automatic SOS feature that shares location with emergency contacts.*

*- 80% of caregivers prefer getting periodic location updates of their loved ones.*

*- 40% requested vibration feedback for alerts, useful for users in noisy environments.*

#### Notable User Feedback & Quotes

*- "I just want an app that tells me ‘Turn left’ instead of a confusing map."*

*- "It should automatically notify my caregiver if I stop moving for too long."*

*- "I need an SOS button that instantly sends my location to my family."*

*- "Navigation apps should be simple, with large text and clear buttons."*

*- "A voice assistant would be helpful since I don’t want to read a screen when lost."*

#### Key Takeaways for App Design

*- SafePath must focus on a simple, intuitive UI with minimal steps.*

*- Emergency response should be automated & fast (auto-SOS, caregiver tracking).*

*- Navigation should prioritize voice guidance and large-text display.*

*- The app should integrate resource mapping for quick emergency access.*

*- Multilingual & accessibility settings should be available.*

### Technology & Tools Used

SafePath used common tools to design and test its user interface. We gathered user feedback with Google Forms and analyzed it using Google Sheets. Miro, MindMeister, and MindMup helped us understand user needs and brainstorm ideas. We created detailed designs and interactive prototypes using Motiff, Figma, and Canva. Adobe XD and Miro helped improve the user experience. All documents, research, and designs are organized in GitHub using a clear format.

SafePath is designed to be easy to use for everyone, including seniors, caregivers, and people with cognitive difficulties. It includes features like high-contrast colors, large text, multiple language options, and voice-guided navigation. These features make SafePath simple to use and effective in emergency situations.
